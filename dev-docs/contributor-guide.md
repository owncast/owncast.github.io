Owncast is a growing open source project giving freedom, flexibility, and fun to live streamers. This is a starting point for anyone looking to contribute in any capacity.

## Code of Conduct

We want an open, welcoming, harassment-free community for everyone, regardless of background or identity. Owncast follows the [Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/). Report incidents to the community leaders at https://owncast.online/contact. All reports are reviewed promptly and fairly, and the reporter's privacy is respected.

## Who is Owncast for?

Owncast is for anyone who needs to provide a live video stream to viewers. The use cases vary widely: companies streaming conferences, churches streaming to parishioners, TV networks bringing content online, and social streamers sharing games or performances. See the Owncast Project Definition for more.

## Roles and responsibilities

Owncast is a [do-ocracy](https://communityrule.info/modules/do-ocracy/). The people who do the work decide how things get done, and the things someone cares enough to work on are the things that happen. A single bug fix is welcome. So is a larger role. If you want more responsibility, say so, because it is hard to guess whether someone is here for an hour or for years.

## Ways to contribute

Open source is not only code. We could use help with:

- Product design (UI/UX). Start small with rough edges and improvements, and take on work you can finish.
- Marketing design: social images, flyers, stickers, swag.
- Social media and marketing. We are not great at being consistently active, and it makes a real difference.
- Content creation: short videos explaining releases or features, and tutorials for different use cases.
- Documentation, from non-technical explanations to technical configuration guides.
- Development. See the Development doc for setup.

### Where to start with design

1. Browse the "style" section of our [Storybook](https://owncast.online/components) and the Design Guidelines page.
2. Check open issues labeled `needs design` on GitHub.
3. Open an issue for something you want to work on.
4. Discuss it on the issue. Once there is agreement, design happens in our [Penpot organization](https://design.penpot.app) (like Figma, but free and open source). Ask Gabe for edit access.
